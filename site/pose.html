not a model

This entry contains no model. It is the organisers' own published baseline with one pose repaired by a tenth of an ångström. It gained more than every pose-selection strategy in the literature clears, because the scorer is a gate: a pose that fails one geometry check is not degraded, it is zeroed.

01

The track

Predict the bound geometry of each blinded compound inside a cytochrome P450, and be scored on LDDT-PLI (how well the protein–ligand contacts are reproduced), BiSyRMSD and LDDT-LP. Thirty entries. The organisers publish a boltz-2-baseline row and, in their tutorial repository, the twenty finished Boltz-2 predictions that appear to underlie it.

Before scoring, every pose is passed through PoseBusters — a battery of 22 physical-plausibility checks in the dock configuration: bond lengths and angles, internal clashes, ring planarity, and minimum distances to the protein, to organic cofactors and to inorganic ones.

The whole result follows from one line of the scorer: POSEBUSTERS_MAX_FAILURES = 0.

02

The gate, and a deficit of 0.079 Å

A pose that fails any one of the 22 checks is zeroed before aggregation — LDDT-PLI 0, LDDT-LP 0, and BiSyRMSD replaced by a 20 Å penalty. Not down-weighted. Not degraded. Zeroed.

Of the organisers' twenty baseline poses, nineteen pass all 22 checks. One — OCNT-2313429 — fails minimum_distance_to_protein: a ligand oxygen sits 2.3589 Å from a protein carbon, a relative distance of 0.7258 where 0.75 is required.

Two caveats this page depends on.

That the board's boltz-2-baseline row is those exact files is an assumption we never verified; our own entry is a cif → pdb conversion of them, and §05 turns up evidence that it may not hold.

And one compound is not 1/20 of the published score. The live board scores only the phase-1 subset of the test set, so on the board this page reports, one failure is worth roughly 10% of the metric rather than 5%.

03

The repair has to move the ligand less than the metric can see

Two constraints pull against each other. The pose must clear a relative distance of 0.75, and it must stay inside LDDT's tolerance, whose smallest distance threshold is 0.5 Å — move the ligand further than that and the repair costs more contacts than the zeroing did.

So: MMFF94 on the ligand alone, with bond orders taken from the challenge SMILES; every protein and heme heavy atom within 6 Å held fixed; a flat-bottom floor at 0.80 × the van der Waals sum on ligand–receptor pairs already close to it; and every ligand heavy atom tethered to its original Boltz-2 position at k kcal/mol/Å².

Provenance, because this report's standard is traceability. Only the k = 20 row is a machine artifact. TETHER_K = 20.0 is a hardcoded constant with no CLI override, so the other three rows record an exploratory sweep and are not reproducible from the committed script. They are shown because they are the reason for the choice, not as evidence.

04

The audit: the risk was concentrated in one check

Before submitting, every thresholded quantity PoseBusters reports was audited across all twenty poses. The margins are comfortable everywhere except one.

Three passing baseline poses sat within 0.025 of the protein-contact floor. The scorer's requirements file does not pin a PoseBusters version and we validated on 0.6.5 — so each of those three was a version bump away from costing ~10% of the metric. The same restrained minimisation was applied to all three: OCNT-2395712 0.7581 → 0.7745, OCNT-2395503 0.7721 → 0.7794, OCNT-2312554 0.7746 → 0.7855. That is submission v2: validator (True, []), PoseBusters 20/20.

The honest cost of that fix, which a “the risk was in exactly one check” story would hide.

Because the floor term skips iron, nothing opposed the ligand drifting toward the heme, and the worst inorganic-cofactor relative distance fell 0.9852 → 0.9352. It is the one check on which v2 is strictly worse than v1. The margin against 0.75 is still 0.185, so this was safe — but it was unrestrained rather than controlled, and a future version should add an explicit Fe term.

05

What the board can and cannot tell us afterwards

Registered before submitting: a range, 0.3950 + 0.02–0.03 ≈ 0.41–0.42, and a point prediction of ≈ 0.4158 — the baseline with the zeroed compound moved to the average of the other nineteen.

Measured: 0.4241. Against the baseline row: LDDT-PLI +0.0291, BiSyRMSD −1.3720 Å, LDDT-LP +0.0752, and rank 15 → 9.

That is the whole of what is established. The per-compound decomposition is not recoverable from the published columns, and the attempt to recover it is instructive enough to record rather than quietly drop.

The obvious inversion — 0.3950 × 20 = 7.900 with one zero, so 0.4241 × 20 − 7.900 makes the repaired compound 0.582 — is wrong, and refutable from the same two board rows. Applied to the other two metrics it returns a pose with LDDT-LP 1.504 and BiSyRMSD −7.44 Å. Both lie outside their own definitions, so the premise fails by a factor rather than a rounding.

The denominator is the error: the live board scores the phase-1 subset, and each column is a mean over 1,000 bootstrap resamples of it rather than an exact mean. Admissibility across all three metrics bounds the scored set at n ≤ 13; at n = 10 the three agree that the repaired pose would be LDDT-PLI 0.291 against 0.4389 for the others.

On every admissible n, the rescued pose is a below-average pose. The gain is the difference between mediocre and zero — not a good pose being thrown away.

The flattering reading is an artifact of the wrong denominator. The registered 0.4158 point prediction rested on that same denominator, so it was mis-specified too: it is not a prediction the measurement can be said to have beaten. This section previously contained the 20-compound arithmetic as a finding. It was removed before publication by an adversarial check against the other two columns of our own board row — the same error this report retracts for the regression track, made again on a different track.

06

What was deliberately not done

Regenerating poses for consensus selection was rejected at submission time. A PXR entrant's published measurements put the noise floor on pose-selection strategies at +0.0138, with only cross-engine agreement (+0.0381) and contact consensus (+0.0220) clearing it. But the organisers' repository ships model_0 only, so consensus means re-running the co-folding and discarding the one pose set whose board score we actually know. It could not have been validated either: 78.9% of CYP3A4 PDB entries fall inside Boltz-2's training set, leaving five post-cutoff structures to measure a +0.02 effect on. Ranking by Boltz confidence is worse than random (−0.0417), and the baseline already is the top-confidence pose.

Coverage 1.0 is not evidence of anything. coverage is set from LDDT-PLI.notna() before the PoseBusters zeroing is applied, so a failed pose still counts as covered — and the baseline row carries coverage 1.0 despite its failure. What establishes that the repair took is the score itself: had the pose still failed it would have been re-zeroed, and we would have scored ≈ 0.3950.

So we measured it, and the answer is interesting in an awkward way. The cross-engine lever is the one strategy whose published effect clears the noise floor, so it was put under test with a second co-folding engine (Chai-1) against the same twenty blinded ligands, under three bars registered before the arm was launched. All twenty ligands ran. All three bars passed. None of them is informative, and each failure mode was found by auditing the arm's own scorer against the arm's own data.

Two of those deserve naming, because both are mistakes that look like results. The catastrophe count was taken at a 4.5 Å contact radius chosen by us; the challenge's scorer passes no radius and runs at OpenStructure's 6.0 Å default, where the count is zero. And the reference pose was pred.model_idx_0.cif — which is Chai-1's generation order, not its ranking: it writes samples in a loop and never renames them by score. The baseline it was compared against is its engine's confidence-ranked best, so the comparison was each engine's best against the other's arbitrary draw, biased toward exactly the disagreement the bar was counting.

Re-ranking by the engine's own confidence does not rescue it either, and that is the sharper finding.

Chai-1's aggregate_score separates a ligand's five samples by 0.0002–0.0056, while the agreement statistic moves 0.19–0.67 across those same five poses. There is no reliable ranking to re-rank by: the reference pose is underdetermined, not merely mis-chosen. More than half the across-ligand variation in the registered statistic is a draw rather than a ligand (ICC 0.441 for one sample, 0.798 for the five-sample mean).

What did survive, and it is the part worth having

The one comparison here that puts like against like is paired within ligand: how far Chai-1's pose sits from Boltz's, against how far Chai-1's own samples sit from each other. A contact-overlap score has no natural zero, so “the engines agree only 0.38” means nothing until you know that one engine resampled on identical input agrees with itself only 0.52.

The effect size is flat while the evidence sharpens by three orders of magnitude — the signature of a real effect rather than a small-sample artifact. CI95 on the final difference is [0.113, 0.174]. So the two engines are genuinely non-redundant: the second one is not another draw from the first's pose distribution, and the lever has a real mechanism behind it.

But the size is +0.14, not the +0.40 that the registered ceiling implied. And the lever's own arithmetic needed correcting in the same direction: its ceiling was written as k × Δ / n, which counts catastrophes over the twenty blinded ligands while dividing by the smaller subset that is actually scored — double-counting the subsetting, and 1.5–2× optimistic. It is k × Δ / 20, independent of n. The baseline's own 0.3950 on these same poses also bounds a flagged pose near 0.21–0.27 rather than 0.15, so Δ is about 0.19, not 0.30.

Registered expectation +0.0381. Defensible range after those corrections: +0.005 to +0.035 — and this arm cannot separate the ends of that range.

The honest close. The arm passes the preconditions it was written to test and the decision still does not follow from them. It has no ground truth on the blinded twenty, so it measures disagreement and infers badness — and disagreement between two engines is symmetric: it never says which one is wrong. Every additional ligand sharpens the count and adds nothing to that inference. The only instrument that can settle whether the lever pays is a head-to-head submission against a known score. That is a decision about submission slots, not about compute, and this page will say which way it went.

+0.0291 LDDT-PLI over the baseline it was built from, with no modelling, by auditing a gate instead of a predictor.

The six-place move should be read knowing that ranks 9–14 span 0.4241 → 0.4014, and four entries sit inside 0.0046 of each other. The score gain clears the published noise floor; “six places” is mostly a property of a dense board.

07

So we spent the GPU day, and the pool refuted the reason for spending it

The section above ends by saying this page would record which way the consensus question went. It went almost entirely nowhere, and the measurement that says so is the one worth publishing.

The remaining uncollected lever was majority-mode selection, published at +0.0546 — but it carries a −0.151 penalty when the mode taken turns out to be the minority, and a mode read off twenty samples cannot tell those two cases apart: 11/20 has a Wilson 95% lower bound of 0.342. So the lever is only collectable at a depth the original arm never reached. Twenty-four L4-hours were spent deepening the Boltz-2 pool from 20 samples per ligand to 60–90, same engine, same ccd: HEM, same --use_potentials, same real MSA, one distinct seed per chunk. 154 chunks, zero failures. A rule registered in advance refuses the swap unless the majority is established (Wilson lower bound above 0.5), the control pose is not already in that mode, and cross-engine agreement does not prefer the control.

Every pre-registered bar passed. One ligand of twenty changed.

The majority mode is established on 10 of 20 ligands at full depth — the bar was ≥8, and a projection made at depth 10 had predicted exactly 10. It did not matter, because established did not convert into different.

Read the third column, not the second. At ten samples per ligand the pool already selects the same pose as the full pool on 18 of 20 ligands; at forty, on all twenty. The pool saturated at depth 40, which means roughly two thirds of the GPU day changed no pose at all — and the deciding question was already answered by the twenty samples the earlier arm had. The honest description of that spend is a negative result bought at full price. It is published here because the same lever is sitting uncollected in other entries' plans, and the depth table is the cheapest way to tell them not to bother.

Why one swap and not five

The dominant reason for declining is not that the majority was unclear. It is that our shipped pose was already the majority mode. On OCNT-2535422, sixty poses formed a single mode. On OCNT-2312554, 87 of 90. The organisers' model_0, which this entry is built from, is already where deep Boltz-2 sampling lands — so consensus selection had almost no error to correct. A pre-run projection expected five swaps; the pool delivered one, and the gap between those two numbers is the finding.

The one swap is OCNT-2395328: 70 poses, 8 modes, a majority of 56 with Wilson lower bound 0.692, and cross-engine agreement independently prefers the new pose (0.465 against the control's 0.394). That second selector has no fitted parameters and did not choose the pose, which is the only reason the swap is taken at all. The other nineteen files are shipped byte-identical to the scored submission, verified by cmp, so a board read is a clean A/B on one ligand rather than a confounded comparison.

What this cannot tell us, stated plainly. There is no oracle on the twenty blinded ligands — nothing in our possession can score a candidate pose. Every number above describes the pool and the rule; none of them prices the candidate. A single changed ligand bounds the expected effect at 1 × Δ / 20, about +0.008 if the swap repairs a bad pose by the Δ ≈ 0.19 this track's own baseline implies — and the same size negative if it does not, because contact disagreement is symmetric and never says which pose is wrong. The live board also scores a subset, so the one changed ligand may not be in it.

Two defects found by measuring rather than assuming

The chain convention was inverted. Our generated poses place the drug in chain B and the heme in chain C, both residues named LIG; the submission that scored 0.4241 is the opposite, HEM in B and LIG in C. Every statistic in the pipeline was unaffected — the heme is identified by its iron, not its label — but a swapped-in pose would have declared its cofactor where the scorer looks for the target ligand, on precisely the ligands the rule changes. The swap is now relabelled atom-for-atom and revoked rather than guessed if the two molecules' atom counts disagree.

The ledger outlived the data it described. Ten ligands' first twenty samples were pushed under an earlier archive naming and were lost with the VM that made them, while the run ledger still recorded them as complete — so the realised depth was 60 where the ledger claimed 80. Depth is therefore reported from the surviving pose files, counted, not from the ledger that claims them.

A throughput number belongs to the hardware it was measured on. Reusing one across GPUs is how a 24-hour window gets pointed at 30 hours of work.

The budget itself came in 2.26× over: the per-chunk rate this window was planned against had been measured on an A100 and the window ran on an L4, at 729–777 s per ten-sample chunk rather than 331 s. Caught by differencing consecutive ledger timestamps on the first fourteen rows. It also made the generation order load-bearing — a clock-terminated run that deepens one ligand at a time leaves half the set shallow, which would have failed the depth bar by queue position and made the table above unreadable. The order is now breadth-first across ligands, so a stop at any instant leaves the pool a readable rectangle.

The last check before the slot

The swapped pose carries the highest internal strain in the set — PoseBusters energy_ratio 2.31 against 1.05–1.79 for the other nineteen. The dock config does not gate on it, so it cannot zero us; but it describes precisely the pose being changed, which makes it worth a look before spending a one-per-team slot. Read alone it is not evidence: the statistic is the pose's UFF energy over the mean of a generated conformer ensemble, so it carries the ligand's own flexibility as well as the geometry — and this is the most flexible ligand of the twenty. Busted paired, in a single process, the comparison inverts: the pose being replaced is the more strained of the two.

The pairing was necessary rather than tidy: the same candidate measured 2.294 in one session and 2.310 in another, because the conformer ensemble is regenerated per process. That is a 0.016 run-to-run drift on a number being read to three decimals, and it is exactly the size of difference an unpaired comparison would have been asked to resolve. Only the within-run difference means anything — and it does not oppose the swap.

The board's answer, and what it closes

Submitted 2026-10-18 17:08 UTC. It scored LDDT-PLI 0.4207 against the banked 0.4241 — −0.0034, rank 13 from 7 — and the entry has been rolled back to the bytes that scored 0.4241. The other two columns disagree with each other (LDDT-LP improved 0.8056 → 0.8090, BiSyRMSD worsened 3.8148 → 3.8422); the scored column is LDDT-PLI, and it says the organisers' model_0 pose was the better of the two. The move is the size the pre-registration predicted for one ligand of twenty, ±0.008, with the sign it could not predict.

So all four bars passed and the board still said no. The bars established that a majority existed, that it was well separated, and that an unfitted second selector agreed with it. None of that is evidence the majority mode is nearer the crystal pose, and with no oracle on the blinded twenty, nothing in our possession could have supplied it. Consensus among samples measures where the model is confident, not where the ligand is.

A lever measured on the board and found negative is a stronger result than the same lever declined on cost.
