01

The problem

Predict how strongly each compound inhibits four cytochrome P450 isoforms, CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4, as a pIC50, for a blinded set of about 750 compounds. Each blinded measurement comes with a credible interval from its fitted dose-response curve.

The metric is the soft-threshold relative absolute error (ST-RAE). A prediction anywhere inside the credible interval costs nothing; outside, it costs the distance to the nearest edge. That total is divided by the same quantity for a predictor that always says the mean, so 1.0 is as good as predicting the mean, and the overall score is the macro average over the four isoforms.

Where this entry ended up, regression track, submission v9:

The models are a LightGBM/XGBoost ensemble and a small multitask MLP, neither of them unusual. The rest of this page is about the two things that mattered more than either model: what the model was trained on, and where its predictions were placed.

02

The defect: every dose-response label is a selected label

The release ships a single-concentration screen at 50 µM, a log2 fold-change, for 4,376 compounds on all four isoforms. A compound gets a dose-response curve, and so a pIC50 label, on an isoform only if it was promoted out of that screen. The promotion rule differs by isoform:

Promotion by isoform, measured on the release
isoformdose-response / screenedwhat decides promotion
CYP1A21,412 / 4,376rises with inhibition; 100 % below log2FC −1.5

A model trained on dose-response labels alone has never seen a CYP2D6 non-inhibitor, and it over-predicts every unselected compound.

The two isoforms whose promotion depends on activity, 1A2 and 2D6, scored worst in this entry’s early submissions: board about 0.88 and 1.46, against 0.52 and 0.57 for the other two.

03

The fix: what one screen readout actually knows

With r = 2log2FC, the fraction of activity remaining, dose-response pIC50 is monotone in r on every isoform. Among the 303 compounds that carry both a curve and a no-inhibition readout (r ≥ 0.9), the median pIC50 is 2.211 and the credible range is about [1.07, 3.52].

So the screened-but-not-promoted compounds join the training set, not as a point at 2.211, which would claim a precision the readout does not have, but as an interval label: the loss is zero anywhere inside the window. For intermediate readouts the window is the 10th to 90th percentile of dose-response pIC50 among compounds with the same readout.

Three higher-ranked entries report the same rows hurting as point labels and helping as bounds. Here, on the validation surface described next, the interval labels were worth −0.243 macro ST-RAE for LightGBM (3/3 seeds) and −0.313 for the MLP (2/2 seeds). CYP2D6 went from 1.51 to 0.60, and its ranking improved too, ρ 0.378 → 0.476, which no placement can do.

04

Validation: the surface is part of the method

The original split, V1, was a scaffold split of dose-response rows only. Every row in it was selected for activity, so it held no non-inhibitors and could not see the defect at all. A selection fix has to look worse on V1: promotion selects on y, the test set’s analog expansion selects on x, and the board scores P(y | x) on the unselected population.

V2 adds held-out screened compounds, scored against their interval labels. V2 reproduces the board isoform by isoform. V1 never did.

Even V2 is not the test set. Nearest-neighbour Tanimoto to the training set needs no labels, and it shows the two are different regimes. The blinded set is an analog expansion bought around the training chemistry, while 82 % of training compounds are Murcko-scaffold singletons (5,370 scaffolds for 6,145 compounds), so a “scaffold split” is close to a random, more diverse partition and asks harder questions than the board does.

05

Placement: the leaderboard as an instrument

A model trained on a selected label set predicts on that set’s scale. The last step is a per-isoform affine map, a centre and a spread, onto the blinded population. The map is monotone, so it cannot change a ranking, only where the predictions sit.

ST-RAE on 750 fixed compounds is deterministic, so the difference between two board rows is a measurement with no sampling noise. Moving one axis per submission turns the leaderboard into a measurement of the metric’s slope along that axis. v5 and v6 moved the spread only; v7 moved the centre.

Convexity brackets the minimum

ST-RAE is a sum of hinge terms, so under an affine map it is convex and piecewise linear in both the centre and the spread. Two consequences were used.

  1. 1 The metric along one axis is a convex, piecewise-linear curve that no one can see. Each submission measures one point on it, exactly.
  2. 2 v5 and v6 widened the spread. The secants between measured points are still negative: the score is still falling, and any forecast past the last point is an extrapolation.
  3. 3 v8 overshot on purpose, on every axis. It scored worse, 0.5520, as expected. But the secant now changes sign, and on a convex curve a sign change brackets the minimum. Every later forecast becomes an interpolation.
  4. 4 v9 aimed at the vertex of the parabola through three measured points. The chord between the two points that straddle it is an upper bound from convexity alone, so each cell’s chord was pre-registered as its bound.
  5. 5 v9 landed under the chord in every cell. The macro point forecast was 0.5236; the board said 0.5232.

One more catch before v9 shipped: writing four decimals collapsed 10–20 distinct predictions per column into ties. Submissions are written at seven, the first precision that is rank-identical to the source.

06

What didn’t move it

After v9 the only lever left was the model. Every candidate was held to a pre-registered bar of +0.005 macro ρ on at least 5 of 6 seeds. Nothing cleared it.

The yardstick matters more than any of these numbers. One fixed GBM, same data, over 18 scaffold splits: macro ρ has sd 0.0323 and ranges from 0.5022 to 0.6344. That is about ten times the effects being argued about, so every comparison is paired within split, over at least six splits, with a sign count. An earlier “0.09 ρ gap” between the MLP and the GBM was that range: the two had been scored on different splits. The report lists every claim withdrawn this way rather than deleting it.
